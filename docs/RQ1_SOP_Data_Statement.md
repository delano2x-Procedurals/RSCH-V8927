## Abstract (overview of the prior work)

This workspace joins two BMGT 8044 Excel workbooks—the course reading list and the Week 7 leadership-theory alignment file—without dropping source fields. The joined tables hold references, theory and dissertation-spine rows, leadership themes, a parking lot, an interview protocol, analysis templates, and citation-insert fields. The study design is generic qualitative inquiry with semi-structured, critical-incident interviews (Caelli et al., 2003; Kahlke, 2014; Tisdell et al., 2025). Stakeholder theory and stakeholder salience—power, legitimacy, and urgency—guide what the questions ask (Donaldson & Preston, 1995; Mitchell et al., 1997). Leadership-theory rows sit beside that spine so prompts can be checked against both governance constructs and leadership standards.

RQ1 asks the researcher to evaluate which data-analysis strategies fit qualitative methods. For this interview dataset, reflexive thematic analysis is the primary fit (Braun & Clarke, 2021; Lester et al., 2020). Theory-informed thematic analysis is the companion, because theory must be named and used on purpose (Bradbury-Jones et al., 2014; Collins & Stockton, 2018). Delve is the computer-assisted qualitative data analysis (CAQDAS) product used for coding, retrieval, memos, and audit (Delve, n.d.; Paulus, 2023; Salmona & Kaczynski, 2016). CAQDAS is the category, not the tool name. Course rows that mention NVivo stay in the archive. Survey scoring and regression stay archived as a classmate comparative plan; they do not analyze these interviews.

The aligned tables do not code the interviews in advance. They make later coding accountable. A question that does not map to a named construct must not be used for extraction (Taquette et al., 2022). A citation insert must carry a `REF-` or `XREF-` ID and APA 7 parenthetical or narrative text (American Psychological Association, 2020). Trustworthiness is checked by returning from a theme to an excerpt, a question, and a source (Korstjens & Moser, 2018; Lincoln & Guba, 1985).

**Keywords:** generic qualitative inquiry; reflexive thematic analysis; stakeholder salience; Delve; CAQDAS; citation insert; RQ1; interview protocol; ethical extraction.

## RQ1

**RQ1:** Evaluate the appropriateness of data analysis strategies for use with qualitative research methodologies.

---

## RQ1 data statement (APA 7)

This statement names the data, the unit of analysis, the allowed strategies, the tool, and the citation rule. It is written so a reviewer can see what the amalgamated dataset is and what it is not (Broom, 2021; Lester et al., 2020).

1. **What the data are.** The working dataset for later interviews will be audio or transcript files from generic qualitative, semi-structured, critical-incident interviews. Until those files exist, the amalgamated workbook and workspace are the preparation dataset: 31 reading-list items, supplemental citations, theory and spine rows, nine leadership themes, parking-lot items, aligned interview questions (IQ-01 to IQ-08), one unaligned template (IQ-09), and citation-insert rows. Each row has a stable ID (`REF-`, `XREF-`, `THEME-`, `PL-`, `IQ-`, `CIT-`, `TH-`, `SP-`, `ARC-`).

2. **What the data are not.** The dataset is not a survey file, not a scored instrument, and not a regression sample. The classmate Qualtrics and linear-regression plan on leadership trust and knowledge sharing is labeled ARCHIVE_COMPARATIVE and is not a source of codes for these interviews.

3. **Unit of analysis.** The unit of analysis is a meaning unit: an excerpt that answers an aligned interview question and can be traced to a named construct (Lester et al., 2020). A theme is a patterned meaning across excerpts, not a count of software nodes (Braun & Clarke, 2021).

4. **Appropriate strategies.** Reflexive thematic analysis is appropriate as the primary strategy because it stays with participant meaning and still allows a named lens to sensitize coding (Braun & Clarke, 2021; Kahlke, 2014). Theory-informed thematic analysis is appropriate as a companion because theory in qualitative work must be visible and bounded (Bradbury-Jones et al., 2014; Collins & Stockton, 2018). Delve is appropriate as the CAQDAS product for storage, coding, retrieval, and memos; it does not replace the method (Paulus, 2023; Salmona & Kaczynski, 2016).

5. **Inappropriate strategies for these interviews.** Simple linear regression, a priori power analysis used in place of information power, and survey scoring keys are not appropriate (Malterud et al., 2016). A forced grounded-theory claim and a phenomenological essence claim are not appropriate unless the protocol and questions are redesigned (Caelli et al., 2003). Unaligned prompts (IQ-09) must not be coded.

6. **Ethical extraction rule.** A review question must test the same construct as its parent question. If the construct is not named, the excerpt is not eligible (Taquette et al., 2022). Participants may withhold restricted artifacts and names. The researcher does not invent codes to fit a preferred theory (Collins & Stockton, 2018).

7. **Citation rule (APA 7).** Every analytic claim that rests on literature must carry a citation insert: `REF-` or `XREF-` ID, parenthetical or narrative form, locator if a page or paragraph is used, and the APA 7 reference (American Psychological Association, 2020). Example parenthetical: (Lester et al., 2020). Example narrative: Lester et al. (2020) described analysis as a starting practice, not a finished finding. Software is cited as Delve (n.d.) when the tool—not the method—is the object.

8. **Trustworthiness.** Credibility is checked with a member-meaning question (IQ-08). Dependability is checked with IDs, Delve memos, and the parking lot. Confirmability is checked by walking backward from theme to excerpt to IQ to REF. Transferability is limited to the spine’s bounds: U.S. SME ITDRPaaS recoverability assurance (Korstjens & Moser, 2018; Lincoln & Guba, 1985; Sinkovics & Alfoldi, 2012).

9. **What alignment does.** Once references, constructs, questions, and citation inserts sit in one set of tables, the full dataset supports ethical extraction. The trail is citation → construct → question → excerpt → Delve code → theme → parked exception. That trail is the data statement’s control. It does not create findings. It keeps findings accountable.

## SOP (standard operating procedure)

**Purpose.** Give one repeatable path from the amalgamated tables to Delve coding and back to a citable RQ1 memo.

**Who uses it.** The researcher (instrument). No first-person voice in write-ups.

**Tools.** This workspace or `workbook/BMGT8044_Amalgamated_Research_Workspace.xlsx`; Delve (n.d.); APA 7 (American Psychological Association, 2020).

### Step 1. Open the joined file

Open the site (README first, Dashboard second) or the Excel twin. Confirm the originals are still in `source/originals/`. Do not recode source cells; add IDs and inserts.

### Step 2. Read the overview

Read the abstract and key-wording table above. Keep generic qualitative inquiry, Delve, and reflexive thematic analysis as the named set (Caelli et al., 2003; Braun & Clarke, 2021).

### Step 3. Find a data point

On the Dashboard, search a title, author, construct, or ID. Store the search (Search, Enter, or a facet). The last three searches stay on that page. Copy the ID (`REF-010`, `THEME-04`, `IQ-02`).

### Step 4. Park anything unfinished

If a decision is not ready, use the parking-lot strip. Give a why, a linked ID, and leave status open. Return later. Do not hide the gap in a theme (Sinkovics & Alfoldi, 2012).

### Step 5. Insert the citation (APA 7)

On any tab, enter the `REF-` or `XREF-` ID, a locator if needed, and where it will be used (IQ, Delve code, RQ1 memo). Save the insert. Copy the parenthetical or narrative text into the memo. Do not paste a URL in place of an APA 7 citation (American Psychological Association, 2020).

### Step 6. Check the interview question before use

Open the Interview protocol tab. Use only rows marked aligned. Confirm the review question tests the same construct. If it does not, write a new `IQ-` ID. Do not extract from IQ-09 (Taquette et al., 2022).

### Step 7. Collect the interview

Use the critical-incident prompt. The researcher is the instrument (Tisdell et al., 2025). Record consent and the interview ID on the analysis-template log. Do not add survey scales from the archived comparative plan.

### Step 8. Load files into Delve

Create or open the Delve project. Import the transcript. Name codes with the workspace IDs when a code maps to a construct (`IQ-02`, `PLU-power`). Delve stores and retrieves. It does not decide the theme (Paulus, 2023; Salmona & Kaczynski, 2016).

### Step 9. Code only eligible excerpts

Read the transcript through once (Lester et al., 2020). Mark a meaning unit only if it answers an aligned question. Enter the excerpt ID, IQ-ID, Delve code, citation insert, and “why eligible” on the excerpt-to-code template. If eligibility is unclear, park it.

### Step 10. Build themes and test them

Group codes into candidate themes. List supporting and disconfirming excerpts. Keep stakeholder salience as a sensitizing lens, not a forced label (Collins & Stockton, 2018; Mitchell et al., 1997). Run the negative-case table. Revise the theme or the protocol. Do not drop the disconfirming row.

### Step 11. Write the RQ1 memo from this data statement

Paste the RQ1 data statement into the course paper or the RQ1 tab memo. Add citation inserts for every literature claim. State that reflexive thematic analysis is appropriate, Delve is the support tool, and regression is not appropriate for these interviews (Braun & Clarke, 2021; Delve, n.d.).

### Step 12. Audit once before a claim is kept

From the claim, walk back: theme ID → excerpt ID → IQ-ID → construct → `REF-` / `CIT-` → Delve memo. If any step is missing, the claim is not ready (Korstjens & Moser, 2018; Lincoln & Guba, 1985).

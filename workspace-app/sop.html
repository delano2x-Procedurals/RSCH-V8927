Abstract (overview of the prior work)

This workspace joins two BMGT 8044 Excel workbooks—the course reading list and the Week 7 leadership-theory alignment file—without dropping source fields. The joined tables hold references, theory and dissertation-spine rows, leadership themes, a parking lot, an interview protocol, analysis templates, and citation-insert fields. The study design is generic qualitative inquiry with semi-structured, critical-incident interviews (Caelli et al., 2003; Kahlke, 2014; Tisdell et al., 2025). Stakeholder theory and stakeholder salience—power, legitimacy, and urgency—guide what the questions ask (Donaldson & Preston, 1995; Mitchell et al., 1997). Leadership-theory rows sit beside that spine so prompts can be checked against both governance constructs and leadership standards.

RQ1 asks the researcher to evaluate which data-analysis strategies fit qualitative methods. For this interview dataset, reflexive thematic analysis is the primary fit (Braun & Clarke, 2021; Lester et al., 2020). Theory-informed thematic analysis is the companion, because theory must be named and used on purpose (Bradbury-Jones et al., 2014; Collins & Stockton, 2018). Delve is the computer-assisted qualitative data analysis (CAQDAS) product used for coding, retrieval, memos, and audit (Delve, n.d.; Paulus, 2023; Salmona & Kaczynski, 2016). CAQDAS is the category, not the tool name. Course rows that mention NVivo stay in the archive. Survey scoring and regression stay archived as a classmate comparative plan; they do not analyze these interviews.

The aligned tables do not code the interviews in advance. They make later coding accountable. A question that does not map to a named construct must not be used for extraction (Taquette et al., 2022). A citation insert must carry a REF- or XREF- ID and APA 7 parenthetical or narrative text (American Psychological Association, 2020). Trustworthiness is checked by returning from a theme to an excerpt, a question, and a source (Korstjens & Moser, 2018; Lincoln & Guba, 1985).

Keywords: generic qualitative inquiry; reflexive thematic analysis; stakeholder salience; Delve; CAQDAS; citation insert; RQ1; interview protocol; ethical extraction.

RQ1

Evaluate the appropriateness of data analysis strategies for use with qualitative research methodologies.

RQ1 data statement

  1. What the data are. Later data will be interview audio or transcripts. Until then, the amalgamated tables are the preparation dataset: references, theory and spine rows, leadership themes, parking-lot items, aligned questions IQ-01 to IQ-08, unaligned template IQ-09, and citation inserts. Each row has a stable ID (Broom, 2021; Lester et al., 2020).
  2. What the data are not. Not a survey file, not scored instruments, not a regression sample. The classmate Qualtrics/regression plan is ARCHIVE_COMPARATIVE only.
  3. Unit of analysis. A meaning unit: an excerpt that answers an aligned question and maps to a named construct. A theme is patterned meaning, not a count of software nodes (Braun & Clarke, 2021; Lester et al., 2020).
  4. Appropriate strategies. Reflexive thematic analysis is primary (Braun & Clarke, 2021; Kahlke, 2014). Theory-informed thematic analysis is the companion (Bradbury-Jones et al., 2014; Collins & Stockton, 2018). Delve is the CAQDAS product for coding, retrieval, and memos; it is not the method (Delve, n.d.; Paulus, 2023; Salmona & Kaczynski, 2016).
  5. Inappropriate strategies. Regression, survey scoring, and power analysis used in place of information power (Malterud et al., 2016). Forced grounded-theory or phenomenological-essence claims unless the protocol is redesigned (Caelli et al., 2003). Do not code IQ-09.
  6. Ethical extraction rule. The review question must test the same construct as the parent question. If the construct is not named, the excerpt is not eligible (Taquette et al., 2022).
  7. Citation rule (APA 7). Every literature-based claim needs a citation insert: REF- or XREF- ID, parenthetical or narrative form, locator if used, and the reference list entry (American Psychological Association, 2020). Example: (Lester et al., 2020). Narrative: Lester et al. (2020) treated analysis as a starting practice. Cite the tool as Delve (n.d.) when the software is the object.
  8. Trustworthiness. Member-meaning check (IQ-08). IDs, Delve memos, and the parking lot for dependability. Walk back from theme to excerpt to IQ to REF for confirmability. Bounds: U.S. SME ITDRPaaS recoverability assurance (Korstjens & Moser, 2018; Lincoln & Guba, 1985).
  9. What alignment does. The trail is citation → construct → question → excerpt → Delve code → theme → parked exception. That trail does not create findings. It keeps findings accountable.

Step-by-step SOP

  1. Open the joined file. Use this site (README, then Dashboard) or the Excel twin. Do not overwrite source cells in source/originals/.
  2. Read the overview. Use the abstract and the key-wording table. Keep generic qualitative inquiry, Delve, and reflexive thematic analysis as the named set (Caelli et al., 2003; Braun & Clarke, 2021).
  3. Find a data point. Search on the Dashboard. Copy the ID. The last three searches stay on that page.
  4. Park anything unfinished. Use the parking-lot strip. Do not hide a gap inside a theme (Sinkovics & Alfoldi, 2012).
  5. Insert the citation. Enter REF- or XREF- ID, locator, and where it will be used. Copy parenthetical or narrative text. Do not replace an APA 7 citation with a raw URL (American Psychological Association, 2020).
  6. Check the interview question. Use only aligned IQ rows. The review question must test the same construct (Taquette et al., 2022).
  7. Collect the interview. Critical-incident interview. The researcher is the instrument (Tisdell et al., 2025). Log the interview ID. Do not add archived survey scales.
  8. Load files into Delve. Import the transcript. Map codes to workspace IDs when they match a construct. Delve stores and retrieves; it does not decide the theme (Paulus, 2023; Salmona & Kaczynski, 2016).
  9. Code only eligible excerpts. Read once, then mark meaning units that answer an aligned question (Lester et al., 2020). Fill excerpt-to-code, including the citation insert. Park unclear rows.
  10. Build themes and test them. List support and disconfirming excerpts. Keep salience as a lens, not a forced label (Collins & Stockton, 2018; Mitchell et al., 1997). Complete the negative-case table.
  11. Write the RQ1 memo. Use this data statement. State that reflexive thematic analysis is appropriate, Delve is the support tool, and regression is not appropriate for these interviews (Braun & Clarke, 2021; Delve, n.d.).
  12. Audit once. Claim → theme → excerpt → IQ → construct → REF/CIT → Delve memo. If a step is missing, the claim is not ready (Korstjens & Moser, 2018; Lincoln & Guba, 1985).
